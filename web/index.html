How it works

Three steps.

  1. Tell it about the family

    Ages, Social Security, savings and spending. A few easy questions.

  2. It plays out 1,000 futures

    Good markets and bad, long lives and short, plus every real year since 1928.

    The one thing that would hurt most, and the smallest change that makes the plan safe.

Questions people ask

Is this financial advice?

No. It's a tool for seeing a plan's weak spots, so a family and their advisor know what to talk about.

Are these real clients?

No. Every family here is made up. The market history is real.

If I put in my parents' numbers, where does that go?

Nowhere. It all runs inside your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere or saved.

What does "89 out of 100" mean?

It played out the plan 1,000 different ways. In 89 of every 100, the money lasted as long as they did.

Can I use it if I'm not retired yet?

Yes. Try it works from age 20. Tell it your pay, what you save each year, and when you'd like to stop working.

Who built this?

Peyton Thalman, a financial advisor who wanted to see why plans break, not just whether they do.
