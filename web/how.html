How it works

Five steps. No jargon.

Here's what happens when you give Glass Brain a family's plan. We'll use Dale and Marilyn as the example.

  1. Step 1

    You tell it about the family.

    Their ages, what Social Security and any pension pay, what they've saved, and what they spend in a normal month. That's it.

    Dale and Marilyn78 and 71
    Social Security$4,050 a month
    Dale's pension$3,100 a month
    Savings$905,000
    They spend$6,500 a month
  2. Step 2

    It plays out 1,000 possible futures.

    Some with great markets, some with terrible ones. Some where they live to 100. For each one, it checks whether the money lasts. Each dot here stands for 10 of those futures.

     

  3. Step 3

    It also replays real history.

    What if they'd retired in 1929? In 1966? In 2000? It runs their plan through every real stretch of markets and prices since 1928. Each square is one starting year.

     

  4. Step 4

    It tests the things that could go wrong.

    A death, a long stay in care, a crash right away, a Social Security cut. One at a time, so you can see which one would really hurt.

  5. Step 5

    It finds the easiest fix.

    It tries the changes the family said they'd be OK with, like spending a bit less in bad years or claiming Social Security later, and keeps the smallest set that makes the plan safe.

    Hoffmans today49
    With one change92

    The change: spend 10% less, only in years the market is down.

The fast part

How it checks 100,000 families at once.

Doing all of that takes the full engine about a tenth of a second per family. Fine for one family. Too slow for a whole firm every time the market moves. So we trained a small AI model to copy the engine's answers, the way a student learns from thousands of worked examples.

about 2.5 hours
