What you're looking at

Neural networks for financial planning, with the glass taken off. You can watch them think, read what they learned, operate on them, audit them, and even write one by hand from the tax code.

1 · The world

A Monte Carlo engine invented 400,000 retirees and lived out each one 256 different ways: random markets, random lifespans, spending cuts in bad years. That's 102 million lives.

2 · The brain

The network saw each retiree's eight facts and the fraction of their lives where the money lasted. Through gradient descent it found its own way to compute withdrawal rate, longevity and crash risk. Nobody wrote those in.

3 · The glass

Every neuron here is real and every connection is a trained weight. Click a neuron to see what it learned to care about. Turn it down and watch the brain's judgement change. That's weight-space surgery.

4 · The tax code

A second brain learned 2026 federal tax law from 3 million returns computed by the MoneyGuide engine, and its neurons bend at the real bracket edges. A third network was written by hand from the statute: 40 neurons, exact to the penny. A law change becomes a weight change.

5 · The engine

A fourth brain learned the whole MoneyGuide planning engine (taxes, RMDs, IRMAA, Social Security) from 60,000 real plans. It answers in microseconds and is checked live against the engine itself.

6 · Why it matters

The brain answers in microseconds. Running a Monte Carlo takes far longer. That's fast enough to X-ray an advisor's whole book on every market tick, and you can see why it said what it said.

Educational model. Real returns are drawn from a simple fat-ish normal market model (stocks 6.5% ± 17%, bonds 1.8% ± 6.5%, after inflation) and Gompertz mortality. It is not financial advice and not any firm's planning engine.
